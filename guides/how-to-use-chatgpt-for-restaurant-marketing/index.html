How to Use ChatGPT for Restaurant Marketing (in 30 Minutes a Week)

By Hero Laboratory · Published 2026-11-12 · Updated 2026-11-12

Quick answer: Use ChatGPT for restaurant marketing by giving it a one-time fact sheet about your restaurant, then batching a week of social posts, turning each special into a post, sign and text, drafting calm review replies and writing catering or event pages – about 30 minutes a week. Always check facts, prices and allergens yourself.

Updated for 2027 · 6-minute read

You know your restaurant needs marketing. Posts, specials, review replies, a newsletter, maybe a catering page. But by the time service is over and the kitchen is clean, the last thing you want to do is stare at a blank caption box.

So marketing gets skipped. The specials board stays the same for a week, a 2-star review sits unanswered, and the place down the street – the one that posts every day – gets the walk-ins on slow Tuesdays. Agencies cost hundreds a month, and hiring a social media person isn't in this year's budget.

Free AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini can't cook or run your floor, but they are very good at one thing restaurant owners never have time for: turning your rough notes into clear, friendly words. Here's how to use them well – in about 30 minutes a week.

Step 1: Give the AI your restaurant's "fact sheet" first

Generic prompts get generic results. Before you ask for anything, paste a short fact sheet into the chat and keep reusing it:

  • Name, neighborhood and type of food
  • Who your regulars are (office lunch crowd, families, students, date nights)
  • Your personality in three words (for example: cozy, funny, no-fuss)
  • Hours, signature dishes and prices
  • Things you never say (for example: "best in town", health claims, anything about allergens you haven't confirmed)

Save this in your phone's notes. Every prompt you run afterward starts with "Use this fact sheet:" and the results will sound much more like you.

Step 2: Batch a week of posts in one sitting

Instead of thinking "what do I post today?" seven times a week, do it once. Try a prompt like this:

Prompt: "Using my fact sheet, give me 7 Instagram captions for next week – one per day. Mix: 2 dish close-ups, 2 behind-the-scenes moments, 1 staff spotlight, 1 customer question and 1 special. Under 50 words each, 3 local hashtags, casual tone."

Then take the photos during a quiet hour and schedule everything with the free scheduler inside Meta Business Suite. Ask the AI to "make number 4 funnier" or "shorter" until it sounds right. Your job is to choose and edit – not to write from zero.

Step 3: Turn specials into posts, signs and texts at once

A single special can become four pieces of marketing. Give the AI the dish, the price and one detail ("slow-cooked for 8 hours") and ask for:

  1. A chalkboard sign under 12 words that can be read from the sidewalk
  2. A social caption with a question at the start
  3. A text message under 160 characters with opt-out wording (if you text customers)
  4. A one-line script your servers can say in under 20 seconds

Same idea, every channel, five minutes. On slow days, ask for "5 promotion ideas for a slow Tuesday that don't rely on deep discounts" – bundles, family nights and local partnerships usually beat 50%-off deals.

Step 4: Answer every review – calmly

Review replies are where AI saves the most stress. When a bad review stings, it's easy to write something you regret. Paste the review and ask:

Prompt: "Write a calm, professional reply to this review. Apologize for their experience without blaming anyone, don't argue about details, invite them to contact us at [email] and keep it under 80 words."

For 5-star reviews, ask for a warm reply that mentions what they ordered. Replying to reviews shows future guests that someone cares – and it only takes a minute once the draft is written for you. Always read the reply before posting, and never let AI invent facts about what happened.

Step 5: Use AI for the "boring" money-makers

Posts get attention, but some quieter pages bring in real revenue. Use your fact sheet to draft:

  • A catering page with three packages and the five questions you need answered before quoting
  • Menu descriptions that make each dish sound as good as it tastes (25 words or less)
  • Your Google Business Profile description with natural local keywords like your neighborhood and cuisine
  • A short monthly email with one new dish, one staff story and one offer

Most restaurants never get around to these. Having them done puts you ahead of a lot of your competition.

What AI should never do for your restaurant

  • Write allergen or dietary information you haven't checked with your kitchen
  • Invent awards, reviews, "voted #1" claims or customer quotes
  • Promise things your team can't deliver (like "ready in 5 minutes" on a Friday night)
  • Receive customer names, phone numbers or payment details – keep private data out of AI chats

Think of AI as a fast assistant who writes first drafts. You're still the owner who decides what goes out.

A simple 30-minute weekly routine

  1. 10 minutes: batch next week's 7 captions
  2. 5 minutes: turn this week's special into a sign, post and server script
  3. 10 minutes: reply to every new review
  4. 5 minutes: one "money page" task – catering, menu or Google profile

It won't make you famous overnight, and no tool can guarantee more customers. But showing up consistently, every week, is what most busy restaurants struggle with – and this routine makes it realistic.

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT write social media posts for my restaurant?

Yes. Give it your restaurant's fact sheet – cuisine, location, hours, best dishes, tone and words you never use – then ask for a week of posts in one go. Edit each post, add your own photos and check every price and date before you publish.

Is ChatGPT free for restaurant marketing?

The free versions of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are enough for posts, specials, review replies and menu descriptions. Paid plans are faster and remember more, but a small restaurant can do all of this with free tools.

How should a restaurant reply to bad reviews with AI?

Paste the review and ask for a short, calm reply that thanks the guest, apologizes for the specific problem and invites them to contact you directly. Read and edit it before posting, and never let the AI invent what happened or promise refunds you didn't approve.

What should restaurants never use AI for?

Never let AI decide allergen or health information, make claims you can't prove such as 'best in town', publish prices or hours without checking, or post fake reviews. Use it to draft words – you stay responsible for every fact.

How much time does AI restaurant marketing take each week?

About 30 minutes once your fact sheet is ready: 10 minutes to batch the week's posts, 10 minutes for specials and signs, and 10 minutes for review replies. Results depend on your consistency and your local market.
